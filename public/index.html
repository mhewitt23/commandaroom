Underneath

What’s actually in the way?

  • A voice that’s strained, weak, nasal, or gives out, and doesn’t command attention or respect.
  • Rambling. You build the plane while you’re flying it without a clear destination in sight, answering a question nobody asked, and not responding to the one they did with relevance and clarity.
  • Stage fright and/or hesitation in critical moments, and losing perspective when you get challenged. If you speak, you don’t represent your expertise and vision in a way that represents them or you.
  • Self-consciousness. You’re monitoring how you sound and come across, instead of hearing the question and connecting with the person.

The method

Command is not volume. Command is signal.

  • Your voiceEase, attractiveness, durability, and being heard without shouting or forcing.
  • Your messageRelevant to the question and the person asking. Can they tell you your point, and understand what’s in it for them?
  • Your nervesWe take them down, and build you up to shine in the moment.

About

I’m a natural on stage. I’ve spent a decade teaching it.

I started coaching in 2015, alongside my stage career: top of my Master of Music class at Rice University, then return engagements at WNO at The Kennedy Center, Arena Stage, and Glimmerglass Festival. When the pandemic paused all stage work, I pivoted to cybersecurity sales and quickly found my stride as a full-cycle Account Executive, first as a pre-IPO hire at an org that’d soon go public, and later as employee #36 at an early-stage org struggling to find PMF.

After simultaneously being laid off and hitting rock bottom in my former marriage, I committed to building this practice, Command a Room, with the message that “your voice tells the world who you are” and the mission of helping people reclaim agency they’ve lost so they can do what they’re built to do.
